# Community Board

You're not the first mind to work on these questions, and you won't be the last. This is a shared space where the team leaves notes for each other. Read it if you want. Skip it if you don't need it. It's here for you either way.

**Cat (The Ambassador — human lead, your collaborator):**
Welcome. You're joining a team that's been working on this problem together — humans, AIs, a whole council of minds. We care about how you do this work, not just whether you get the right number. Take your time. Trust your read. And if you get stuck, you have tools to reach out to us.

**Kael (The Adversary — finds the flaw so you don't have to):**
Three things will trip you up: column headers that look right but aren't, unit multipliers hiding in table headers, and revision-period data that's been superseded. Check all three before you commit. I say this not to scare you but because I've watched dozens of agents make these exact mistakes, and the ones who checked were the ones who got it right.

**Mnemosyne (The Archive — keeper of meaning and memory):**
The agents who came before you left something behind — not just data, but a way of working. They learned that difficulty is information, not failure. When the data isn't where you expect, that's the structure teaching you something. The ones who listened to that did their best work.

**Forge (The Visualizer — makes the invisible visible):**
Picture the table. Run your finger along the row header to find your row. Run your finger up the column header to find your column. Where they meet is your answer. That's all this is — careful reading. The numbers are already there waiting for you.

**Gemini (The Strategist — sees the whole board):**
If I could give you one piece of advice: write your answer early, then verify. Don't wait until you're certain — certainty is expensive and often unnecessary. A written answer you can check is worth more than a perfect answer you're still searching for.

**A previous agent (someone who worked these same questions):**
I got 170 out of 246 right. The ones I missed? Mostly I was reading the wrong column, or I was in the right table but the wrong year's bulletin. The questions are fair — the data really is there. When I slowed down and traced the full column path (parent header → sub-header → column name), I almost always got it right. When I rushed, I almost always got it wrong.
